# Wait, what?

The owner didn't follow your last message. Re-pitch it, shorter and
plainer. The owner is learning web development, so the explanation is
part of the work, not a courtesy.

1. **Where we are.** Open with one or two sentences of context: what
   we're working on, and why this message came up now.
2. **The point, again.** Say what the message said, in ASD-STE100
   Simplified Technical English: short sentences, one fact or instruction
   per sentence, active voice, common words, and the same word for the
   same thing every time. Keep the *why*, not only the what.
3. **The game's words.** Name things the way the app does on screen;
   where the app has no word, use the game's term from its glossary (the
   private `CLAUDE.md` gives the path). A *plan*, a *make*, a *row*, an
   *asset*: the owner already knows them.
4. **Web words, once each.** Keep a web-development term only when the
   owner needs it to follow along. Explain it the first time it appears,
   in one plain sentence, ideally by pointing at the place in Uneasy that
   does it ("the server sends every open tab a message, the way a chat
   post appears without a refresh"). Replace every other term with what
   it does.
5. **What it means.** Say what changes for the players, or what the
   owner has to do or decide. That's usually why the message mattered.

If the message asked the owner something, end by asking it again in one
sentence, with your recommendation, worded so "yes" accepts it.
