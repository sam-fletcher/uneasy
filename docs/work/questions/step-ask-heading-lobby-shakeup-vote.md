---
title: "Should the lobby, the Shake-Up and the ending vote open with an ask like \"Your move — …\"?"
---

The Play piece turns the open step's heading into the current ask ("Your move — …", "Waiting on otto — …")
and deletes the Waiting On bar. The lobby, the Shake-Up and the ending vote are not plan steps, so the
rulings don't say whether they take that grammar.

Each opens with its own heading today ("Is everyone here?", "You're seated. Nothing to do yet.", "Open spend",
"Announce a spend", "How does the game end?"), and each still sits under the Waiting On bar, which names who
the game waits on. I restyled the headings and left the words and the bar alone.

When the bar goes, these screens lose their only plain statement of who the game is waiting on: the lobby's
"Gathering players" and "1 more player to join", and the vote's "Waiting On: alice, carol". The clocks in the
player row stay, and the vote and the open spend already name the stragglers in their own text.

- **Leave the headings** (built). The words under each heading and the clocks say who is waited on.
- **Use the ask grammar**, for example "Your move — start the Prologue" for the host and "Waiting on alice — she
  starts the game" for a guest; "Your move — vote on how the game ends". This is a copy change in three files.
